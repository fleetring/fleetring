# ADR-0047: Online delegation: the control plane records a child agent's lineage, then signs its narrower, shorter-lived JWT with its own issuer key, and revoking any ancestor ends the subtree below it

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-12, by the owner, with open questions 1 to 6 as recommended)
- **Date:** 2026-11-11 (revised three times on 2026-11-12, after an independent security review, its re-review, and a final check)
- **Related:** ADR-0017 (control state), ADR-0020 (simulation; injected randomness), ADR-0025 and ADR-0026 (principals, scopes, tokens; amendments proposed here to 0025 points 3 and 5 and 0026 points 2, 3, 5 and 6), ADR-0035 and ADR-0040 (the Raft control group; ADR-0040 points 2 and 4 are Proposed), ADR-0037 (the router, a `forward_tokens` relay), ADR-0038 (fleet features), ADR-0039 (dev mode; amendment proposed to points 1, 2, 3 and 5), ADR-0041 (the MCP server), ADR-0043 (agent identities; amendment proposed to points 8.3, 8.5 and 8.7), ADR-0044 (umbrella accepted; its §3, specification S1, is deferred after 0.8 by this record; amendment proposed to §9.1), ADR-0046 (operation IDs and their retries), ADR-0048 (Proposed: OpenShell as the agent launcher's sandbox backend); tickets FR-089 (#139), FR-088 (#138, the `grant` action and the grant step), FR-090 to FR-093 (#140 to #143), FR-094 (#144, the workflow experiment), FR-096 (#146, the upgrade from 0.7); [CLI style guide](../cli.md) §16

This record decides how a parent agent gives a child agent a credential in 0.8: by asking the control plane, which checks the parent's live token, narrows exactly, records the delegation and its lineage in control state, and only then signs a JWT for the child. It replaces, for 0.8, ADR-0044's specification S1 (offline biscuit tokens), which stays Proposed and is deferred until the evidence of point 9 exists.

ADR-0044's accepted umbrella still holds (points 1 to 4 there):

- a child is `agent-<parent-id>.<segment>`, and `.` has been reserved for lineage since 0.7 (§1.4, §1.8);
- a child never holds more than its parent, or for longer (§2.4, §3.10);
- `grant` covers a pattern (§2.2);
- revoking a parent ends its descendants.

## Context

**What 0.8 needs.** A parent agent spawns children, each with its own databases under `agent-<parent>.<segment>-` and a credential that reaches those, and whatever slice of the parent's the parent chooses to hand on, and nothing else. Today only the authority's key mints a token, and that key belongs on no node (ADR-0025 point 2). In dev mode a process can mint from the local authority (ADR-0043 point 8.6), but against any other fleet a parent can't hand a child a narrower credential.

**Why online issuance is enough for 0.8.** A design review found that S1's offline delegation hadn't earned its complexity: a biscuit dependency of about 85 crates, holder keys, a custom verifier, self-signed client certificates behind a new TLS setting, and new revocation semantics. The argument for doing it online instead:

- **An agent needs the fleet to use its databases at all.** A child that can't reach the control plane can't create a database, fork one, or locate a primary either. Minting offline buys nothing for 0.8's single-fleet, same-machine workflows.
- **Online issuance can check what offline issuance can't.** The control plane checks the parent's live token, its expiry and the denylist, at the moment it mints. It records lineage in control state, which makes "a principal may revoke what it and its descendants issued" a rule the control plane can check (FR-093). It orders every issuance against every revocation in one control log (point 5). And it needs no new token format: nodes already verify JWTs (ADR-0026 point 5).
- **Offline earns its place only on evidence.** FR-094 runs one real parent and child workflow and answers, with measurements, the questions in point 9 that would bring S1 back.

**What exists** (checked against `main` on 2026-11-12):

- **Tokens** are a strict JWS: header `alg`, optional `typ`, `kid`; claims `iss`, `sub`, `jti`, optional `iat`, `nbf`, `exp`, `fleetring.scopes`; unknown fields refused; `EdDSA` only, from a key ID derived from an Ed25519 certificate in the node's `[tls] ca` bundle (the first 12 bytes of the SHA-256 of the raw public key, base64url); at most 30 days; at most 8 KiB (`MAX_TOKEN_LEN`). JWT times are whole seconds. `TokenSigner::issue` writes `iat` equal to `nbf`, so a token signed with a future `nbf` claims a future `iat`. Tokens may also be minted by standard JWT libraries holding the authority's key (ADR-0025 point 3), which usually write `iat` as the signing time and may backdate `nbf`.
- **The connection rule.** A token must name the principal of the connection's client certificate, unless that principal's `[[principal]]` entry sets `forward_tokens`, as the router's does (ADR-0026 point 3, ADR-0037 point 2). The router verifies a token before any call and forwards it unchanged; it keeps no denylist, so data nodes decide (ADR-0037 point 18). `resolve_identified` also accepts a token on a connection with no client certificate (`Peer::Plaintext`) when the node has a TLS identity; point 4.1 closes that.
- **The denylist** is control state, `Revocations`, a `BTreeMap<Revoked, Timestamp>`: `Revoked::Token(TokenId)` or `Revoked::Key(KeyId)` mapped to the time the entry may be forgotten after (`until`), which a second revocation of the same entry only raises (`Revocations::insert`). It records nothing about who added an entry. Token entries are kept 30 days and 30 seconds; a key's may last for ever. The whole list rides on every heartbeat reply, within the 64 MiB `MAX_MESSAGE_BYTES`. A data node refuses every token once its list is older than the control plane's heartbeat timeout, and rechecks open streams every `STREAM_RECHECK` (1 s) (ADR-0026 points 6 and 7, and its amendment). A control node reads its own state and treats it as always current (`auth::boundary`'s `Source::Control`). A data node **skips** an entry it can't read, with a warning (`revocations_from_proto`); a `Revocation` whose `oneof` case it doesn't know decodes with the case absent, since prost skips unknown fields, so it is skipped the same way.
- **Scopes** (FR-088): the grammar and the lattice (`auth::scope`, `auth::scope::lattice`) are pure code in `fleetringd`, depending only on `changes::matcher` and `fleetring_control::DatabaseName`. `grant_step` narrows silently, and `grant_step_strict` refuses with `NotCovered` naming the first requested scope it would narrow or drop, or `NoGrant` when the holder has no `grant` scope. `fleet` is never kept.
- **Control time is monotonic.** `apply` runs a command at its *effective time*, `max(command time, State::now)`, so time never goes backwards in control state, across a leader change included. It can run ahead of the wall clock when a leader's clock did.
- **Operation IDs** (ADR-0046 point 5): 16 random bytes the client chooses, with requester-scoped, intent-compared resubmission (`OperationNotYours`, `OperationConflict`).
- **Agent identities** (ADR-0043 point 8, FR-081): a directory per agent with a certificate for `agent-<id>`, a token, and `identity.toml`. `agent-<id>` has no `[[principal]]` entry, so the certificate alone authorizes nothing (ADR-0043 point 8.7).
- **Dotted service names exist.** Fleets from before 0.7 may have principals such as `workload` and `workload.prod`, which keep working as services (ADR-0044 §1.8).

Words: this record uses the [lexicon](../lexicon.md)'s *delegated agent* (in prose, a *child*), *root agent*, *delegation issuer*, *issuer certificate*, *delegation ID*, and *lineage*, each added as planned. An *agent name* is a *root agent name*, `agent-<id>` with `<id>` matching `[a-z0-9_]{1,32}`, or a *delegated agent name*, a root agent name followed by one to eight `.<segment>`, each segment `[a-z0-9_]{1,32}`, at most 64 bytes in all. A name is *at or below* `Q` when it is `Q` or starts with `Q` followed by `.`. A *root lineage* is a root agent name and every name below it.

## Decision

### 1. The delegation issuer: one key per control node, certified by the authority

1. **Each control node holds a *delegation issuer key*:** Ed25519, its own, never copied to another node, never in control state, and never logged. `fleetring cert issuer --name <name> [--ttl <d>] --dir <authority dir> [--out <dir>]` creates it where `ca.key` is, writing `issuer.<name>.crt` and `issuer.<name>.key` (`0600`). Each run makes a new key, so renewing the certificate rotates the key.
2. **The authority certifies it with an *issuer certificate*.**
   - Its only subject alternative name is the URI `fleetring://<fleet>/issuer/<name>`, a new subject kind beside `node` and `principal`. `<name>` has the node ID grammar and is, by convention, the control node's ID.
   - It lasts 30 days by default, as a node certificate does, and **at most 90 days** from its start to its end: `cert issuer --ttl` refuses more, counting any backdating of the start within the 90 days. Nodes allow one day of slack beyond 90 days for a certificate another authority tool backdated (point 1.4). The cap bounds how long a stolen key reaches when nobody notices.
   - It is signed with Ed25519. It is not an authority: basic constraints, if present, say so. Its key usage, if present, is digital signature alone. It has **no extended key usage extension** at all, so no TLS stack accepts it as a client or server certificate.
   - *Note (2026-11-12, after the review of S1a, S1b and S2):* "no TLS stack accepts it" is inaccurate. rustls-webpki reads a certificate without an extended key usage extension as good for any use, client and server authentication included. What keeps an issuer certificate from opening a connection is point 1.5: `peer_of` refuses the issuer subject kind on every listener and on a node's client side, and a dialing node's server-name check fails against its URI-only subject alternative name, which FR-090's `issuer_certificate_cannot_open_a_connection` pins. The decision is unchanged; only the reason given here was wrong.
3. **Configuration.** The control node's `[control] issuer_cert` and `issuer_key` name the two files. Both or neither; on a node that runs no control plane, either is a configuration error. At load and at every reload, the certificate must pass every check of point 1.4 that doesn't depend on a token (issued by an authority in `[tls] ca`, the subject, not an authority, no extended key usage, the critical extensions, and the 90-day cap with its slack), and must match the key. At load a failure stops the node, naming both files; at reload the node keeps the pair it had and logs why. The pair reloads on change, as node certificates do, and the node warns from 7 days before the certificate expires.
4. **Nodes need no new configuration.** Every delegated JWT carries its issuer certificate in the JWS header's `x5c`. A node, data or control, verifies, in order, before it trusts anything else in the token:
   1. `x5c` is an array of exactly one base64 DER certificate;
   2. it was issued directly by a trusted authority in the node's `[tls] ca` bundle, and its signature is Ed25519;
   3. its subject URI is `fleetring://<this fleet>/issuer/<name>`, and it names nothing else;
   4. it isn't an authority; it has no extended key usage extension; its key usage, if present, is digital signature alone; and it marks no extension critical other than basic constraints, key usage, and subject alternative name;
   5. its validity period is at most 90 days plus one day of slack, and it is valid at the node's time, with the 30 seconds of leeway that `nbf` has (ADR-0026 point 5) on its start;
   6. `kid` is the ID of the certificate's Ed25519 key, derived as every key ID is;
   7. the JWS signature verifies with that key;
   8. `Revoked::Key(kid)` isn't listed.

   *Note (2026-11-12, after the review of S1a, S1b and S2):* point 8 is extended: a node also refuses the token (FR-090, stage S4b) when `Revoked::Key` lists the ID of the trusted authority key that signed its issuer certificate, so revoking an authority key ends the issuer certificates it signed as well as the root tokens, without waiting for the `[tls] ca` bundle to change. A certificate whose subject key is itself a trusted authority's key is refused at point 2, so one key never signs as both. Both are refusals beyond the text above, never acceptances. A descendant minted from the copy such an issuer signed, by an issuer another authority certified, names the first issuer's key in its chain but not the authority, so after revoking an authority key, also revoke the issuer keys it certified.

   *Note (2026-11-12, after security review R1):* nodes refuse two more issuer-signed tokens, both beyond the text above and neither an acceptance (`docs/spec/tokens-v1.md` §7.1, check 4). A token whose `exp` is after its issuer certificate's end is refused, as point 6 already has the signer refuse it, so a token signed with a stolen key just before the certificate ends can't keep an open stream alive past it. And a token whose first chain entry's `kid`, the key that signed its root token, isn't a trusted authority key is refused, so removing an authority from the `[tls] ca` bundle ends its root tokens' descendants as well as the root tokens; `Revoked::Key` of the authority's ID is still the way to retire one, since it ends them at once on every node, whatever bundle a node holds.
5. **An issuer certificate never opens a connection.** `peer_of` refuses the issuer subject kind, as a client certificate and as a node certificate, with "an issuer certificate can't open a connection". A node or client certificate in `x5c` is refused.
6. **A control node signs only with a key it can use.**
   - **Without `[control] issuer_*`,** `DelegateToken` answers `FAILED_PRECONDITION` with "this fleet has no delegation issuer; run `fleetring cert issuer`". Each control node needs its own pair, since any of them may lead.
   - **It refuses before recording anything** (point 5) when its issuer key is on the denylist, when its certificate has expired, or when the certificate expires before the token would. Each is a `FAILED_PRECONDITION` naming the file and `fleetring cert issuer`.
7. **Dev mode creates one, and keeps it current.**
   - `fleetring dev` writes `tls/issuer.dev.crt` and `tls/issuer.dev.key` from the dev authority when they are missing, valid for 90 days, and the configuration it generates names them. For a dev directory 0.7 wrote, whose `fleetringd.toml` doesn't name them, `[control] issuer_*` default to those two files when `dev = true`, so the configuration file is never rewritten.
   - **Replacement.** At start, and at every check the node makes for a changed certificate, a dev node replaces the pair when fewer than 30 days remain, when the certificate has expired, or when the key doesn't match the certificate. It writes both to temporary names in `tls/`, syncs them, and renames the key, then the certificate, into place. A reload that finds a pair that doesn't match keeps the pair it had and tries again at its next check, so a reader between the two renames never signs with a mismatched pair.
   - That is an exception to ADR-0039 point 5's "never overwritten", and a harmless one: tokens signed with the old key carry the old certificate, so replacing the files invalidates none of them before the old certificate expires. The dev-directory check allows both files and their temporary names.
   - It also amends ADR-0039 point 1, "nothing in the daemon branches on dev mode": a running dev node reads the dev authority's `ca.key` to replace its issuer, and `dev = true` chooses the issuer's default paths. Both stay confined to the issuer files; nothing else in the daemon branches on dev mode, and a node not in dev mode never reads `ca.key`.

### 2. What a delegated JWT holds

1. **The header** is `{alg: "EdDSA", typ: "JWT", kid, x5c: [issuer certificate]}`.
2. **The claims:**
   - `iss`, the fleet, as today;
   - `sub`, the child principal;
   - `jti`, the child's *delegation ID* (point 2.4);
   - `iat`, the effective time of the `RecordDelegation` command that recorded the delegation (point 5.6), in seconds, rounded down;
   - `nbf`, the later of the `not_before` the handler resolved (point 5.4) and `iat`, so `iat ≤ nbf` always; and `exp`, as resolved;
   - `fleetring.scopes`, the scopes as granted, in canonical form;
   - `fleetring.kind = "agent"`;
   - `fleetring.owner`, the caller's `fleetring.owner`, so every agent in a lineage answers to the same principal (ADR-0044 §1.3);
   - `fleetring.parent`, the caller's principal;
   - `fleetring.chain`, the *lineage*: one entry per ancestor, root first, ending with the parent, each `{ id, kid }`, where:
     - `id` is the ancestor's token ID: for the root, its JWT's `jti`; for a delegated ancestor, its delegation ID;
     - `kid` is the key that signed the copy of that ancestor's token from which this token was minted: the authority's key for the root, an issuer key for a delegated ancestor.
3. **The chain is built from the caller's token.** A child's chain is its caller's chain followed by `{ caller's jti, caller's kid }`, where the caller's `kid` is the one in the header of the copy the caller presented. A root token has no chain, so a root's child has exactly one entry. A child minted by issuer B from the copy of `42.c7`'s token that issuer A signed therefore has the chain `[{root jti, authority}, {c7's delegation ID, A}]`.
4. **The delegation ID is the delegated token's `jti`.** It is 128 random bits, drawn by the proposing control node from its injected generator (ADR-0020) and carried in the command, as ADR-0046 point 1.1 draws incarnations; printed as 22 base64url characters, within the token ID grammar. `apply` refuses an ID already recorded, and the proposer draws again. It is stable across re-signing (point 5.7): every copy of one delegation has the same `jti` and the same claims.
5. **Depth is at most 8 levels below the root,** independently of the 64-byte bound on names: a delegated token's chain has at most 8 entries. A depth-8 chain and the certificate fit well within 8 KiB, which FR-090 checks.
6. **Root agents' tokens.** A root agent's token, minted with the authority's key by `agent add`, `agent renew`, or dev mode's `mcp --agent`, gains `fleetring.kind = "agent"` and `fleetring.owner` (and the `grant` scopes FR-092 sets) once every member reports `delegation` (point 6.4). Authority-signed tokens never carry `x5c`, `fleetring.parent`, or `fleetring.chain`. An authority-signed token with `kind = "agent"` must name a root agent name; a node refuses any other. **Without `kind = "agent"`, a token can't delegate.** `token issue` gains no way to set `kind` in 0.8.
7. **`iat` is when the token was signed.** From 0.8 FleetRing's signers write `iat` as their clock's reading at signing time, apart from `nbf`; `TokenSigner::issue` changes accordingly (FR-093), since `iat = nbf` lets a token signed with a future `nbf` claim to be issued after any revocation. Every node refuses a token, root or delegated, whose `iat` is later than its own time plus 30 seconds, the leeway `nbf` has. Requiring `iat ≤ nbf` of every token was rejected: a token another JWT library minted with the authority's key, with `iat` its signing time and a backdated `nbf`, works today, and would stop working on an upgrade no feature gates. Issuer-signed tokens, which are new, do satisfy `iat ≤ nbf` (point 3.8).
8. **Unknown header fields and claims are still refused.** A 0.7 node refuses every token with `x5c` or the new claims, failing closed.

### 3. What every node enforces for an issuer-signed token

An issuer key may mint only tokens inside the *agent tier*. This is the constraint ADR-0044 §6.1 attaches to an issuer key as a trust anchor, fixed in code here rather than configured. A node refuses an issuer-signed token unless all of the following hold, each refusal with a named, lowercase reason:

1. `sub` is a delegated agent name;
2. `fleetring.kind` is `"agent"`, and `fleetring.parent` is `sub` minus its last segment;
3. `fleetring.chain` has exactly one entry per level of depth (one per `.` in `sub`), at most 8, each with an `id` and a `kid` that are valid IDs;
4. `fleet` is not among the scopes;
5. every scope's pattern lies within the root's namespace, `agent-<root>-` or `agent-<root>.`, where `<root>` is the first segment of `sub`: covered by `Prefix("agent-<root>-")` or `Prefix("agent-<root>.")`;
6. every `grant` pattern lies within `<sub>.`;
7. every pattern passes the separator refusal for new token forms (ADR-0044 §2.3): before its `*`, it ends in a byte that isn't an ASCII letter or digit;
8. `iat ≤ nbf < exp`, and `exp − nbf ≤ 24 h`;
9. `iss` is the node's fleet.

*Note (2026-11-12, after the review of S1a, S1b and S2):* `_` is both a segment character (ADR "Words") and, not being an ASCII letter or digit, a separator for point 3.7. So `admin:agent-42.c7_*` passes the separator refusal, yet it covers the sibling `agent-42.c7_x` and its children's databases, not only names `c7` would mean. It stays inside the root's namespace, so point 3.5 holds and no lineage escapes its root, and a `grant` scope must still lie within `<sub>.` (point 3.6). The separator refusal guards against names a careless prefix covers by accident; it doesn't confine a scope to one subtree, and scopes ending in `_*` should be read that way. `docs/spec/tokens-v1.md` (S1c) states it with a vector.

And the converse: a token without `x5c`, which the authority signed, is refused if its `sub` is a reserved dotted agent name (one that starts with `agent-` and contains `.`), as 0.7 already refuses (ADR-0044 §1.8), or if it carries `fleetring.parent` or `fleetring.chain`. A service name such as `workload.prod` is unaffected. A token with `x5c` whose `sub` isn't a delegated agent name is refused by point 3.1.

**What nodes can't check, the control plane does at issuance.** That a child's scopes are a narrowing of its parent's, that its `exp` is no later than its parent's, and that `owner` is the parent's are facts about the parent's token, which a node doesn't see. Point 5 enforces them when it mints, and `apply` again against a delegated parent's record (point 5.9). This is why a stolen issuer key reaches the agent tier, not one lineage (point 8).

**Revocation is checked against the lineage, whatever key signed what.** A node refuses a delegated token if any of the following is on the denylist it holds:

- its own `jti` (`Revoked::Token`) or `kid` (`Revoked::Key`);
- any `id` in `fleetring.chain` (`Revoked::Token`);
- any `kid` in `fleetring.chain` (`Revoked::Key`);
- `sub`, or any ancestor name derived from `sub`, under a `Revoked::Principal` entry (point 11) whose `before` is at or after the token's `iat`: the token is refused when `iat × 1000 ≤ before`, `before` being in milliseconds.

A root token is checked for its own `jti` and `kid`, as today, and its `sub` under `Revoked::Principal` with **30 seconds of leeway**: it is refused when `iat × 1000 ≤ before + 30,000`. The leeway covers a root token's signer whose clock ran up to 30 seconds ahead of control time (see "Assumptions"). A delegated token needs none, since its `iat` is control time. A token without `iat` counts as issued before every `before`, so it fails closed.

**An unreadable denylist is no denylist.** From 0.8, a node that meets a denylist entry it can't read, whether its `oneof` case is absent, unknown to it, or malformed, treats the whole list as unusable: it refuses every token with `UNAVAILABLE`, as it does for a stale list (ADR-0026 point 6), until a list it can read wholly arrives, and logs which entry it couldn't read. Skipping the entry, as `revocations_from_proto` does today, would let a node that missed `Revoked::Principal` accept every token it should refuse. Certificates keep working, as for a stale list.

The same checks run on the `STREAM_RECHECK` path, so an open stream below a revoked ancestor ends within a second of the node hearing of it. The audit line of a delegated request names the principal, the parent, the chain's token IDs, and the issuer key's ID, and never token bytes.

So revoking a root token, any ancestor's delegation, any issuer key that signed an ancestor's copy, or any ancestor principal ends the whole subtree below it (for a root token judged by principal, under the clock bound the security analysis states). That holds when a child minted by issuer A has a grandchild minted by issuer B after a leader change or a key rotation. **This is a release blocker for 0.8.0** (FR-090, FR-093), stated as invariant I5 in point 13.

### 4. How a delegated agent connects

1. **The connection rule depends on the signer.**
   - **An issuer-signed token** is accepted on a connection whose client certificate names `sub` or an ancestor of `sub`: the certificate's principal followed by `.` is a byte prefix of `sub`. So `agent-42`'s certificate carries `agent-42.c7`'s and `agent-42.c7.r1`'s tokens.
   - **An authority-signed token,** a root agent's or any other, keeps today's rule: the certificate must name `sub` itself. The ancestor match never applies to it, so `workload`'s certificate never carries `workload.prod`'s token.
   - **Relay-carried.** A token is *relay-carried* when the connection's certificate names a principal the two rules above don't let carry it, and that principal sets `forward_tokens`. Such a token is accepted as any token is (ADR-0026 point 3), except where point 4.5 refuses it.
   - **A token needs a client certificate.** From 0.8, a token on a connection with no client certificate is refused with `UNAUTHENTICATED`, "a bearer token needs a client certificate", whatever signed it. Today `resolve_identified` (`auth::boundary`) accepts a token on `Peer::Plaintext` whenever the node has a TLS identity; the gRPC listener requires a client certificate, so only a listener that skips one could reach that path, but nothing would stop it, and every token would then be a bearer token. FR-090 makes `resolve_identified` refuse it, with a test.
   - Any other connection is refused.
2. **The child runs where its root's certificate is.** `--agent 42.c7` uses `agent-42`'s certificate and key with the child's token (point 10, ADR-0043 point 8.3 as amended). No certificate or key is written into the child's directory.
3. **No certificate is ever issued for a dotted name.** 0.7's refusal stands (ADR-0044 §1.8).
4. **The root's certificate key alone authorizes nothing,** since `agent-<id>` has no `[[principal]]` entry (ADR-0043 point 8.7). It is, however, what turns any token of its lineage into a usable credential on a direct connection (point 8).
5. **`DelegateToken`, and `RevokeToken` under the ancestor rule (point 11), refuse a relay-carried token,** root or delegated, with `PERMISSION_DENIED`: "delegation needs a direct connection with the certificate of the caller or an ancestor". Minting and the ancestor rule's revocations therefore need proof of an ancestor's certificate key, whatever relays a fleet runs. **One exception:** a relay-carried token may revoke its own `jti` under the ancestor rule, so `agent renew` behind a gateway can revoke the token it replaces; a relay that abuses this ends only tokens it was handed. `RevokeToken` with `fleet` keeps today's rule.
6. **Under an agent launcher.** With ADR-0048's OpenShell backend (Proposed), a sandboxed agent's token reaches the fleet only through the router, injected by OpenShell's supervisor, so it is relay-carried. A sandboxed agent therefore can't delegate or revoke under the ancestor rule from inside its sandbox, which is what ADR-0044 §11.2 asks: children are spawned by the launcher, outside the sandbox, which holds the root's certificate and calls `DelegateToken` on a direct connection. Where ADR-0048 has its credential proxy attenuate a token, in 0.8 the proxy asks `DelegateToken` for a narrower one, since biscuit attenuation is deferred. ADR-0048 point 4's daily token push relies on S1's bearer self-delegations; in 0.8 the push carries delegated JWTs instead, which last at most 24 hours and cross the router as any token does.
7. **What this gives:** a child's token copied without an ancestor's certificate key is useless on a direct connection. Relay-carried, it is a bearer token for its own scopes until it expires (at most 24 hours, 2 hours by default) or is revoked, as every token is today; point 8 and the security analysis say so.

### 5. `Control.DelegateToken`

1. **The call.** `Control.DelegateToken { operation_id, segment, scopes, lifetime, not_before }`; empty `scopes` asks for the defaults. The response carries the token, its delegation ID, the principal, the scopes, `not_before`, `expires_at`, whether the lifetime was capped, and the ID of the issuer key that signed it. Its `Scope:` line is "an agent's token whose grant covers the child (checked in the handler)".
2. **The caller.** The caller `P` is authenticated by its token, never by a certificate alone, and the token isn't relay-carried (point 4.5). `P`'s token must carry `kind = "agent"`, and `P` must be an agent name. The child is `P.<segment>`, with `segment` matching `[a-z0-9_]{1,32}` and the child's name at most 64 bytes, at most 8 levels below the root.
3. **Scopes.**
   - **Explicit scopes** pass FR-088's **strict** grant step against `P`'s effective scopes and `grant` patterns: a requested scope that isn't fully covered is refused, naming it (`NotCovered`), and so is a caller with no `grant` (`NoGrant`).
   - **The defaults** are the parent's own actions on the child's prefix: the *narrowing* grant step of `{admin:<child>-*, stream:<child>-*}`. A read-only parent therefore gives `read` and `stream`, never `admin`. The defaults hold nothing over `<child>.*`: no `admin` over the databases of the child's own children and no `grant`, so a default child can't delegate. An empty result is refused, as with "your token holds no grant over agent-42.c7-".
   - **Either way, the result must satisfy point 3.4 to 3.7 for the child's name,** or the request is refused, naming the first scope that doesn't. The grant step alone doesn't ensure it: a parent holding `grant:agent-42.*` could otherwise hand a child `grant:agent-42.*`, which point 3.6 refuses on every node, and a root token with an operator-chosen scope outside its namespace could otherwise pass one on.
4. **Times.**
   - The handler resolves `not_before` and `exp` to absolute times before it proposes the command, which carries them, reading "now" as the later of its own clock and `State::now`. When `State::now` leads its clock by more than 30 seconds (a leader with a fast clock was replaced), it refuses with `UNAVAILABLE`, "control time leads this control node's clock by <n> s", rather than mint a token every node would refuse as not yet valid.
   - `not_before` defaults to now. An explicit one earlier than now less 30 seconds is refused, and so is one more than 24 hours after now, or at or after `P`'s `exp`. So every delegated token expires within 48 hours of its recording.
   - **The default lifetime** is `min(2 h, P.exp − not_before)`, capped silently; the response says when it was capped.
   - **An explicit lifetime** longer than `P.exp − not_before`, longer than 24 hours, or shorter than 60 seconds is **refused**, never capped.
   - `exp = not_before + lifetime`, so the child's `exp` is never later than `P`'s.
5. **Recording, then signing.** The control node records the delegation in control state *before* it signs (`Action::RecordDelegation`, point 6): its delegation ID and its **complete claims**, `iat`, `nbf`, `exp`, scopes, chain, owner, and parent. It then signs exactly the claims the committed command recorded, with its own issuer key, and returns the JWT. If signing fails after recording, a retry with the same operation ID re-signs; if no retry comes, the record expires harmlessly.
6. **`iat` is the command's effective time** (Context), rounded down to the second, and the recorded `nbf` is raised to it if the resolved `not_before` was earlier, which point 5.4 keeps to a fraction of a second. Control time never goes backwards, so a delegation recorded before a revocation in log order always has `iat` at or before that revocation's `before`.
7. **Retries re-sign; they don't promise identical bytes.**
   - **The canonical request** is the segment, the scopes as given (parsed and normalized, so their order doesn't matter; empty stays empty), the lifetime as given (or absent), `not_before` as given (or absent), and the caller's own token ID.
   - **A retry** with the same operation ID, from the **same authenticated principal**, with the **same canonical request**, returns a token with the **same delegation ID and identical claims**, signed by whichever control node answers. Ed25519 is deterministic only for one key and one message, so after a leader change or a key rotation the signature, `kid`, and `x5c` may differ. On the same control node with the same key, the bytes are identical. Both copies are one delegation: revoking its ID ends both.
   - **A retry re-signs only a live delegation (point 5.9) that the denylist doesn't refuse.** Otherwise it is refused, naming why: "this operation's delegation has expired", or "this operation's delegation is revoked" (the operation's tombstone, point 6.2). It never mints again.
   - **`RecordDelegation`'s `apply` is idempotent too.** A known operation from the same requester with the same canonical request changes nothing and returns the recorded delegation, so two racing retries, or a retry read from a lagging control node, record once.
   - **Persisting the issued token bytes was rejected.** Control state is replicated, snapshotted, and backed up, and a stored bearer credential would be readable wherever those copies go.
8. **Retries are scoped to the requester.**
   - The same operation ID from another principal is refused with `PERMISSION_DENIED` (`OperationNotYours`), revealing nothing about the operation.
   - The same ID with a different canonical request is refused with `ALREADY_EXISTS` (`OperationConflict`), naming the first field that differs.
   - **An operation's record lasts as long as its delegation's record, plus 1 hour; once the delegation is revoked, 1 hour from the revocation** (point 6.2). A retry after the delegation expired or was revoked, within that hour, is refused as above. After it, the ID is unknown, and the request is a new one, which records a new delegation: the old one is no longer usable, so nothing usable is duplicated.
9. **What `apply` checks.** `RecordDelegation`'s `apply` refuses, in the same step that records, a delegation that fails any of the following. Every check is on identifiers and values the command carries; `apply` authenticates nothing and never sees a token.
   - **Structure, whoever proposed it.** `requested_by` is the parent principal; the child's name is the parent's followed by one segment, an agent name at most 8 levels below the root; the chain's length is the child's depth; `iat ≤ nbf < exp`, `exp − nbf ≤ 24 h`, and `nbf ≤ iat + 24 h`, so point 6.3's bounds hold whoever proposes; the scopes are in canonical text (point 6.5) and satisfy point 3.4 to 3.7 for the child's name.
   - **Against a delegated parent's record.** The child's `owner` is the parent record's `owner`; the child's chain is the parent record's chain followed by `{parent's delegation ID, caller's kid}`; the child's scopes pass the strict grant step against the parent record's scopes; and the child's `exp` is no later than the parent record's `exp`. So a planted child whose chain omits its parent can't be recorded, and every delegated child dies with `Revoked::Token` of its parent. A root parent has no record, so for it the handler's checks (point 5.3) are the only narrowing check, and `Revoked::Principal` of the root is the remedy if a compromised leader planted children under it.
   - **Revocation.** The caller's own token ID, any `id` or `kid` in the caller's chain, the caller's `kid`, the caller's principal, or any ancestor of it is revoked in the current state (for `Revoked::Principal`, by point 3's comparison, with the leeway for a root caller).
   - **The child's own name, revoked in the same second.** A `Revoked::Principal` entry covering the child's name whose `before` is at or after the child's `iat × 1000` refuses the command with `UNAVAILABLE`, retryable (point 11.5), since every node would refuse the token it would sign.
   - **Forged lineage.** The caller is delegated, and its own delegation ID isn't recorded and live, or the claims it presented differ from the recorded claims for that ID. The command carries the caller's presented claims, every claim but `kid` and `x5c` (which belong to a copy), or a digest of their canonical encoding, which `apply` compares with the recorded ones.
   - **Limits** (point 5.10).

   A delegation is *live* while its record is kept (point 6.1) and its `exp` is after the command's own stamped time; a `nbf` in the future doesn't make it less live, though nodes refuse the token until then. The control log totally orders this command against every revocation:
   - a revocation applied first makes the delegation fail;
   - a revocation applied after it covers the new child through its `chain`, or through `Revoked::Principal`, whose `before` is at or after the child's `iat` (point 5.6).

   A token minted with a stolen issuer key under a fabricated delegation ID, or under a real ID with claims the control plane didn't record, can't obtain descendants.
10. **Limits.** Beyond any of these, `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`:
    - at most 256 live delegations per root lineage; a delegation revoked by ID or by principal has its record pruned (point 11.4), so it frees its slot at once;
    - at most 30 delegations per minute per caller principal, and at most 60 per minute from the delegated agents of one root lineage. The lineage rate never counts or refuses the root agent itself, whose own 30 a minute still applies, so a lineage's descendants can't stop their root, and a lineage records at most 90 a minute. Both are counted in `State`'s rate windows (point 6.2), over the commands' effective times, so a leader whose clock lags can't reopen a window, new callers in one lineage can't multiply the rate, and pruned records don't reset it;
    - depth at most 8 levels below the root, within the 64-byte name.
11. **The feature check.** While any member lacks `delegation`, the call is refused with `FeatureUnsupported`, naming the member and its release, and nothing is logged (ADR-0038 point 4).
12. **The token never reaches a log, an error, or a metric label.** The audit line names the caller, the child, the scopes, the expiry, the operation ID, the delegation ID, the issuer key's ID, and the result.

### 6. Lineage in control state, and the `delegation` fleet feature

1. **`State::delegations: BTreeMap<DelegationId, Delegation { principal, parent_principal, parent_token, chain, claims, operation, requested_by, recorded_by, kept_until }>`.**
   - `claims` holds every claim but `kid` and `x5c`, so any control node can re-sign the delegation.
   - `recorded_by` is the leader that proposed the command. The log layer stamps its leader ID (ADR-0040 point 2) into the command as it stamps time, so replay reads it from the log and stays deterministic; nothing a client sends sets it. It is **advisory against a compromised leader**: Raft followers don't check an entry's leader ID against the node that sent it, so a compromised leader could stamp another's. Incident response therefore lists by time window as well as by `recorded_by` (point 6.4). **In 0.8,** before FR-064's Raft group, the single writer stamps its own node ID beside the time it stamps (`Command::new`), so with one control node it is always that node; FR-064's leader stamps its own the same way.
   - A record is kept for a duration counted from its command's effective time: `kept_until = effective time + (exp − command time) + 30 s + 1 h`. The duration keeps a control clock that ran ahead from shortening a record below its token's life, and the hour is a margin against a leader's clock stepping forward later (see "Assumptions"). Expired records go after `kept_until`, and point 11.4 prunes a record earlier when a revocation covers it.
   - The map is default-empty and skipped when empty, so a state without delegations is written byte for byte as before.
2. **`State::delegation_operations: BTreeMap<OperationId, { requested_by, canonical_request, delegation_id, outcome, kept_until }>`.** `outcome` is the recorded delegation, or a *tombstone*, "revoked", which point 11.4 writes when it prunes the delegation's record. An operation is kept until its delegation's `kept_until` plus 1 hour; a tombstone is kept 1 hour from the revocation that wrote it, however far off the delegation's `kept_until` was.
   - **Rate windows.** `State::rate_windows: BTreeMap<RateKey, VecDeque<Timestamp>>` holds, for each key, the effective times of the commands counted in the last 60 seconds, at most the limit's number. A key is a kind (delegation, or ancestor-rule revocation) and a scope (a caller principal, or a root lineage's delegated agents). Times older than 60 seconds, and keys left empty, are pruned. The windows are counted by commands, not by records, so a loop whose revocations prune its records still meets the rate. A refused command adds no time. The map is default-empty and skipped when empty, as `delegations` is.
   - **Pruning doesn't wait for `Tick`.** The control node submits `Tick` only after a tick interval with no commands (`control/failover.rs`), so a busy control plane may never tick. `RecordDelegation` and `RevokeToken` therefore prune expired records, operations, tombstones, ancestor-rule entries and rate-window times in their own `apply`, as `revoke_token` already calls `forget_through`; and the control node's timer proposes a `Tick`, whose `apply` prunes the same, when the earliest `kept_until` or ancestor-rule `until` passes, as its fork driver does for fork deadlines, so an idle lineage's records go too. A refused request records nothing, since a rejection leaves the state untouched (ADR-0038 point 4); its retry is judged again. The map is separate from ADR-0046's `fork_operations`, so one operation ID in both means two operations.
3. **Bounds.** Per root lineage, at most 90 delegations a minute are recorded (point 5.10), and at most 256 are live at once. The records still kept are the live ones, plus those within their margin (about an hour) after expiry: at most the 256 live when the hour began and the 5,490 recorded during it, so about 6,000 in all. The operations are those records' operations for another hour, about 11,400, plus tombstones from the last hour, at most about 5,700, so about 17,000. Both bounds come from the 256 limit, the lineage's rates, and the 24-hour bound on `not_before`, whatever revocations do, since a revocation only removes records and its tombstones last an hour.
   - **Revoked delegations don't hold records alive.** Minting children and revoking them by principal, even one principal entry raised in place again and again, keeps no records: each `Revoked::Principal` or `Revoked::Token` prunes the records it covers in the same `apply` (point 11.4). So the cap on unpruned records that the implementation plan suggested, revoked ones included, is subsumed by pruning and the rates.
   - **A backstop all the same:** `apply` refuses a `RecordDelegation` with `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` while its lineage holds 8,192 unpruned records. That is above the about 6,000 the rates allow, and pruning in `apply` (point 6.2) keeps expired records from piling up on a fleet too busy to tick, so it never fires while pruning and the rates work as specified, and it keeps a bug in either, or a later change to a rate, from growing records silently. The replay test checks that it never fires under the rates.
4. **What the records give:**
   - idempotency (point 5.7);
   - the "issued by" relation that the ancestor rule checks (point 11);
   - the forged-lineage refusal (point 5.9), for delegation and for revocation alike;
   - **a minimal listing for incident response:** `Control.ListDelegations { recorded_by, recorded_from, recorded_to }`, which needs `fleet`, returns live records (principal, delegation ID, parent, chain, `iat`, `exp`, `recorded_by`), never tokens, and filters by the recording leader and a window of effective times. After a compromise, list the whole window the node may have led, not only its `recorded_by` (point 6.1). `fleetring agent list --fleet [--recorded-by <node-id>] [--since <time>]` prints it. A listing for agents without `fleet` is not part of this decision.

   No node's refusal depends on a record: nodes check the chain and the denylist. What depends on a record is the control plane's own decisions: delegating from a token, and revoking a delegation, or its principal, under the ancestor rule. `Revoked::Principal` of the caller itself is the handle that never depends on any record but the caller's own (point 11).
5. **Scopes in control state have one implementation.** Delegation records hold scopes, and `apply` checks them (point 5.9). Writing a second, text-level checker for `apply` was rejected: it would be a second implementation frozen into the log, and a later release that read scopes differently would make replicas diverge. Instead:
   - the scope grammar and lattice move from `fleetringd::auth` into `fleetring-control`, with the database matcher they use, as ADR-0044 §9.2 already places control-state types there; the handler and `apply` call the same pure functions (amending ADR-0044 §9.1);
   - the grammar's canonical text is normative: `<action>:<pattern>` or `fleet`, with the actions and the pattern grammar of ADR-0044 §2 and the separator refusal; `apply` refuses any scope whose text isn't the canonical form of what it parses to;
   - the golden vectors of ADR-0044 §2.4 (in `docs/spec/tokens-v1.md`) pin the results, and the replay test covers them;
   - **any change to the grammar or the lattice that changes whether `apply` accepts a command is a fleet feature** (ADR-0038 point 4), so a mixed control group never applies the same command two ways.
6. **The `delegation` fleet feature** (ADR-0038 point 4) gates `RecordDelegation`, the `Revoked::Principal` variant, and the ancestor rule's revocations (point 11). The first accepted command that uses any of them enables it.
   - **A node reports `delegation` only once it enforces all of point 3 and point 11:** the issuer path, the lineage checks, `Revoked::Principal` with its leeway rule, and the unreadable-denylist rule. That is FR-093's change, not FR-090's or FR-091's: a member that reported the feature while ignoring `Revoked::Principal` would break I5. 0.8.0 isn't cut between FR-090 and FR-093.
   - **Mixed control groups.** That a control node of the previous line can't apply `RecordDelegation`, and that a voter lacking the feature holds it back, rest on ADR-0040 point 4 (the feature gate covers the control group), which is Proposed; this record depends on it being accepted before 0.8.0.
   - Once enabled, a member that lacks it can't join, and `upgrade --rollback` to 0.7 is refused, naming it.

### 7. Credentials stay out of model context

- No MCP tool returns or accepts a credential, and none delegates or revokes: a test asserts the tool list has no tool named `*delegate*` or `*token*`.
- Delegation is a CLI operation of the harness that starts the agent (`agent delegate`, point 10).
- `mcp --agent <child>` serves as the child with its own token, which the server holds and never prints.

### 8. What a compromise reaches

| Compromised | What it reaches |
|---|---|
| An issuer key | The agent tier, through forged tokens: any delegated agent name, with scopes inside its root's namespace, for at most 24 hours a token, until the key is revoked (`Revoked::Key`) or its certificate expires (90 days at most, 30 by default). A forged token is usable on a direct connection with its root's certificate key, or relay-carried. It can't delegate or revoke under the ancestor rule, since it isn't recorded (point 5.9, point 11.3). Revoking the key ends every token it signed, forged or legitimate, and every descendant minted from a copy it signed. It doesn't end copies of the same delegations that other keys signed, if any exist, nor descendants minted from those. Nothing outside the agent tier: no root token, no certificate, no `fleet`, no other database. |
| A control node | Its issuer key, as above, plus everything a control node already decides. As the leader: any control command, within what `apply` checks (it can record delegations for any caller it names, but only ones point 3 and point 5.9 allow; `recorded_by` is stamped by the leader, so it is advisory here, point 6.1), and the denylist every data node receives, so it can withhold revocations. It never holds the authority's key. |
| A relay's certificate key (a router's) | Every token presented to it, or that it can see, for that token's scopes until it expires or is revoked: it can replay them as any relay can carry them. It can revoke a token it was handed by that token's own `jti` (point 4.5), which ends only that token and its descendants. Never delegation or any other ancestor-rule revocation. |
| A root agent's certificate key | Nothing alone. With any token of its lineage, the root's own or a descendant's: that token's scopes on a direct connection, minting children within it if it holds `grant`, and the ancestor rule's revocations within that token's subtree. |
| A child's token | Nothing on a direct connection without its root's certificate key. Relay-carried, the child's scopes, until it expires or is revoked; never delegation. |
| A parent's token | The parent's scopes, and, with its root's certificate key, minting children within them until it is revoked. Every such child is recorded below the parent, so revoking the parent's token ID or principal ends them all (point 11). |

*Note (2026-11-13, after security review R2):* "every descendant minted from a copy it signed" names that key only if a resend can't hand a copy an existing child. Point 5.7 compared a resend's requester and canonical request, but not which copy of the caller's token it presented, so a copy of a delegated parent's exact claims re-signed with a stolen issuer key could resend an operation the genuine copy had used and receive that child, whose chain names the genuine copy's key, not the stolen one. Revoking the stolen key wouldn't end it. A resend is therefore refused, `ALREADY_EXISTS` naming the caller token's signing key (`RequestField::CallerKey`), when the `kid` of the copy the caller presents differs from the last chain link's `kid` in the delegation's record: only the copy the child was minted from receives it again, and a new operation from another copy records a new child whose chain names that copy's key. The control node's handler and `apply` share the check (`State::delegation_resend`). A legitimate parent whose own token was re-signed after a rotation resends with the copy it first used, or asks under a new operation ID. This narrows point 5.7's retries; it accepts nothing new.

`SECURITY.md` says so when FR-090 ships. The [security analysis](#security-analysis-what-each-compromise-reaches-and-what-each-revocation-ends) below gives the reasoning, the assumptions, and what remains.

### 10. The CLI

[`docs/cli.md`](../cli.md) §16 designs these, planned for 0.8.0:

- **`cert issuer --name <name> [--ttl <d>] --dir <authority dir> [--out <dir>]`**, point 1; `--ttl` is at most 90 days.
- **`agent delegate <child-id> [--scope <s>]… [--ttl <d>] [--not-before <time>] [--operation-id <id>] [-o table|json]`.** The parent is the child ID minus its last segment and must be an identity in the agents directory. The command writes the operation ID into the child's directory *before* it calls, and resends with it on `UNAVAILABLE` or a lost reply, so a re-run after a crash never records a second delegation. It writes the token and `identity.toml` (version 2, `issued_by = "delegation"`, with `parent`, `root`, `owner`, the delegation ID, and the operation ID), and no certificate or key.
- **`--agent 42.c7`** resolves to the root's TLS directory, `<agents dir>/42/`, as `agent-42`, with the token in `<agents dir>/42.c7/token`. `agent add 42.c7` is refused, naming `agent delegate`.
- **`agent list` and `agent show`** show lineage: the parent, the owner, the root, the delegation ID, the issuer key's ID, and the operation ID. **`agent list --fleet`** lists the fleet's live records, with `fleet` (point 6.4).
- **`agent renew <child-id>`** delegates again as the parent, with the recorded scopes and a new operation ID, then revokes the old delegation ID (point 11). Renewing a parent never renews its children. **`agent renew <root-id>`** revokes the token ID it replaces, as ADR-0043 point 8.7 already has it do, now under the ancestor rule when the caller lacks `fleet`. If a revocation is refused, the new token is still written, and the command says when the old one expires.
- **`agent remove <child-id>`** revokes `Revoked::Principal` for the child as its nearest local ancestor identity, ending it and every agent below it, then deletes the directory. **`agent remove <root-id>`** works without `fleet` when the agent revokes itself, and revokes both its principal and its own token ID (point 11.7). After a root's principal revocation, the command prints when a replacement root token may be minted: `before` plus 30 seconds, from the reply.
- **`token revoke --principal <name>`**, and the ancestor rule for `token revoke --token-id` and `--principal` run with `--agent <id>`.

### 11. Who may revoke what: the ancestor rule and `Revoked::Principal`

1. **`Revoked::Principal { fleet: Option<FleetRef>, principal, before }`.** It refuses every token, root or delegated, whose `sub`, or any ancestor name derived from `sub`, is at or below `principal`, and whose `iat` is at or before `before` (point 3's comparison: exact for delegated tokens, with 30 seconds of leeway for root tokens). It matches `agent-42` and `agent-42.c7`, never `agent-420`. `fleet` is always `None` in 0.8, present so the shape never changes (ADR-0044 §3.8). It needs `delegation`. In the protocol it is a new `oneof` case of `Revocation` carrying `before_ms`, which a node that can't read it handles as point 3's unreadable-denylist rule says.
2. **Entries record how they were added.** From 0.8, `Revocations` maps each `Revoked` to an entry `{ until, added }`, where `added` is either `Fleet` (with `fleet`, or before 0.8: every existing entry reads so) or `Ancestor { root, by }`, the root lineage and the caller principal that added it under the ancestor rule; a principal entry also holds its `before`. The denylist keys a principal entry by `(fleet, principal)`: a later revocation of the same principal raises its `before` and its `until` in place, never adding a second entry, and an entry added with `fleet` stays `Fleet`. The new fields are written only once `delegation` is enabled, so the state is unchanged before. Every cap and rate below is enforced in `apply`, on this state.
3. **Who may revoke what.** The `Scope:` line of `RevokeToken` becomes "fleet, or the ancestor rule".
   - **With `fleet`:** anything, as today. A principal revocation of a dotted service name's parent, such as `workload`, also matches `workload.prod`; the CLI says so.
   - **The ancestor rule applies only to agents, and only to callers the control plane can vouch for.** The caller `P` is authenticated by its token, which isn't relay-carried (point 4.5, except for its own `jti`). `P` is an agent name, and either a root agent with an authority-signed token, or a delegated agent whose delegation is recorded and live and whose presented claims equal its record, which **`apply` checks** as point 5.9 does for delegation. A token forged with a stolen issuer key therefore can't revoke anything, however its `jti` is chosen. Every target must be an agent name at or below `P`, so a service such as `workload` can never end `workload.prod`'s tokens.
   - **`Revoked::Token(id)`:** if `id` is the token `P` presented (its root token's `jti`, or its own recorded delegation ID), or if `id` is the ID of a recorded delegation whose principal is below `P`, which means `P` or one of `P`'s descendants issued it. An unknown or pruned `id` is refused with `NOT_FOUND`, "no live delegation with that token id", so nobody can probe for, or revoke, another principal's root tokens.
   - **`Revoked::Principal { principal: Q }`:** if `Q` is `P` itself, ending `P` and its subtree, or `Q` is the principal of a recorded delegation below `P`. A name nobody delegated can't be listed.
   - **`Revoked::Key`** stays `fleet`-only.
   - Every refusal is `PERMISSION_DENIED`, naming the rule. The audit line records the caller, the target, and whether the authority came from the ancestor rule or from `fleet`.
4. **A revocation replaces what it covers, and prunes the records it ends.** In the same `apply`:
   - **Only a principal entry replaces other entries.** An ancestor-rule `Revoked::Token` entry for a delegation stores that delegation's principal, since the record that said whose it was is pruned in the same `apply`. A principal entry for `Q` replaces every `Ancestor` entry it covers: `Revoked::Principal` entries for names below `Q`, and `Revoked::Token` entries whose stored principal is at or below `Q`. It covers them because each was added earlier, so everything they refuse is at or below `Q` with `iat` at or before the new `before`. `Revoked::Token` entries for root tokens are never replaced, since a root token's `iat` comes from its signer's clock and only its ID is a clock-free handle (point 11.7). `Fleet` entries are never replaced. The new entry's `until` is at least the longest `until` it replaces.
   - **A token entry replaces nothing.** Deciding "below `d`" by name would be wrong: with a segment reused, a grandchild named below `d1` can descend from `d2`, and replacing its entry would revive it. A principal entry has no such problem, since it refuses by name and time, and every token a replaced entry refused is at or below `Q` and was recorded before it.
   - **Every `Revoked::Token` and `Revoked::Principal`, with `fleet` or under the ancestor rule, prunes the records it ends:** every delegation record whose ID or chain names the revoked token ID, or whose principal is at or below `Q` (a delegated record's `iat` is always within `before`), goes, and each pruned delegation's operation gets the "revoked" tombstone, kept 1 hour from this revocation (point 6.2). Pruned delegations stop counting toward the 256 at once, and can't delegate again, since they are no longer recorded. `Revoked::Key` prunes nothing, since it ends copies, not delegations.
5. **`before` and `until` are set by the control node, never by the caller;** the request has no field for `before`, and an ancestor-rule caller none for `until`.
   - **`before` is the revoking command's effective time,** stored as it is. Nodes add 30 seconds when they judge a root token (point 3); a delegated token, whose `iat` is control time, is judged exactly. A delegation ordered after the revocation in the log is refused by `RecordDelegation`'s own check (point 5.9) if the revocation covers its caller; one ordered before it has `iat ≤ before` (point 5.6). **Re-delegating a revoked name in the same second:** `agent remove 42.c7` followed within a second by `agent delegate 42.c7` would record a child whose `iat`, in whole seconds, is at or before the entry's `before`, so every node would refuse it. `apply` refuses such a `RecordDelegation` instead, with `UNAVAILABLE`, "agent-42.c7 was revoked less than a second ago; retry", which the CLI's resends retry; a second later it records a token that works. Raising the child's `iat` past `before` was rejected: an `iat` ahead of control time could then fall after a later revocation's `before`, which breaks I4. The reply carries `before`, so a CLI that revoked a root principal can say when a replacement root token may be minted: 30 seconds after `before`, by the signer's clock.
   - **`until`, for a principal entry:** for a root agent name, 30 days, 30 seconds, and the 1-hour margin from the effective time, the longest a root token may live; for a delegated agent name, the longest `kept_until` among the records it covers, plus 30 seconds. `kept_until` already holds the 1-hour margin (point 6.1), and every delegated token it can refuse belongs to one of those records.
   - **`until`, for an ancestor-rule `Revoked::Token`:** the longest `kept_until` among the delegation and the records below it, plus 30 seconds, for a delegation ID; the presented root token's `exp`, counted as a duration from the effective time, plus 30 seconds and the 1-hour margin, for the caller's own root token.
   - With `fleet`, `RevokeToken` keeps today's rule for `until` (ADR-0026 point 5), except that `until` is never earlier than the longest `kept_until` among the records it prunes, plus 30 seconds: a short `--ttl` would otherwise let a token revive once the entry is forgotten, with no record left to revoke it by.
6. **Bounds on what the ancestor rule adds,** beyond which `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`:
   - **A cap of 1,024 live `Ancestor` entries per root lineage.** Every ancestor-rule entry counts, a delegated agent's revocation of itself included. The cap refuses only a revocation that would raise the lineage's count. So it never refuses one that raises an existing key in place, nor one that replaces at least one existing entry, which every ancestor's principal revocation over a subtree that holds entries does (point 11.4). It never refuses a root agent's revocation of its own principal, which replaces every delegated entry in its lineage, nor of a root token it presented. When it does refuse, the message names the caller's child whose subtree holds the most entries, so the caller can revoke that child, which frees them. (*Amended 2026-11-13 by the owner (R3 finding 1, option b):* entries the root adds have their own budget of 1,024 per lineage, separate from the 1,024 its descendants share; I11's bound is 2,048 plus exempt entries. An entry counts in its adder's budget, and one raised in place keeps its first adder's. The cap refuses only a revocation that adds more new entries to the caller's budget than it replaces from that budget, so descendants never block the root, and a root at its own cap that would replace only its descendants' entries is refused. A full root budget's refusal says so and suggests waiting for its entries to expire, revoking the root itself, or asking an operator with `fleet`; a full descendants' budget's still names the busiest child.)
   - **At most 30 ancestor-rule revocations per minute per caller principal, and 60 per minute from the delegated agents of one root lineage,** counted in `State`'s rate windows (point 6.2). The lineage rate never counts or refuses the root agent itself, whose own 30 a minute still applies, so a lineage takes at most 90 a minute. A caller that exhausts its own rate never stops another caller, and descendants that exhaust the lineage's rate never stop the root or an operator. An ancestor between them can still be held up by its descendants' use of the lineage rate; the root, or an operator, revokes the descendants doing it. (*Amended 2026-11-13, R3 finding 2:* a root agent's revocation of its own principal or of the root token it presented is neither counted nor refused by its rate, as the cap already exempts it. Each ends the token that asked, so it limits itself, and counting it let 29 earlier revocations split `agent remove <root>`, landing the principal and refusing the token ID, which the dead token could then never retry.)

   A descendant can't block an ancestor: whatever entries a descendant adds lie in its own subtree, and the ancestor's revocation of that subtree replaces them all. A loop that delegates and then revokes children is held by the rate windows (point 5.10), which count commands rather than records, and by this cap, which its self-revocations count toward.
7. **`agent remove <root-id>` revokes the root's own token ID as well as its principal.** `Revoked::Token(root jti)` ends the root token and, through every descendant's chain, the whole subtree, independently of any clock (see "Assumptions"). (*Amended 2026-11-13, R3 finding 2:* both targets go in one ancestor-rule command, so they land together or not at all; a token the principal covers, a delegation recorded at or below it, gets no entry of its own.)
8. **A child can't revoke its parent or a sibling,** and revoking across fleets isn't in 0.8.

### 12. Upgrading from 0.7

1. **0.7 tokens and identities keep working.** JWTs without the new fields verify as before (their `iat`, equal to their `nbf`, is never in the future beyond the leeway once they are valid), and version 1 `identity.toml` still serves. Existing denylist entries read as added with `fleet`.
2. **While the fleet is mixed,** `DelegateToken` and ancestor-rule revocations are refused with `FeatureUnsupported`, naming the 0.7 member. `agent add` and `agent renew` mint FR-081's defaults without `kind`, `owner`, or `grant`, and say that the agent can delegate after `agent renew` once every node runs 0.8. A token with `grant` or the new claims sent to a 0.7 node is refused there, failing closed.
3. **Nodes report `delegation` from FR-093's build** (point 6.6); a pre-release build with FR-090 to FR-092 but not FR-093 reports nothing, so no fleet enables the feature on a node that ignores `Revoked::Principal`.
4. **Once every node runs 0.8:** give each control node an issuer (`fleetring cert issuer`, then `[control] issuer_cert` and `issuer_key`; the files reload without a restart), and run `agent renew` for each root agent that should delegate. `agent renew` upgrades only an identity whose scopes are exactly 0.7's or FR-081's defaults; scopes chosen with `--scope` are never widened.
5. **The first `agent delegate`** (the first `RecordDelegation`), the first `Revoked::Principal`, or the first ancestor-rule revocation enables `delegation`. Rollback to 0.7 is allowed until then and refused after, naming the feature. FR-096 tests both, alongside the `fork` feature.

### 13. The invariants the tests check

Each is a property of the replay test, the simulation (ADR-0020), or an integration test, as FR-090 to FR-093 name them.

- **I1. Narrowing.** Every recorded delegation's scopes are exactly what point 5.3 computes against the caller's effective scopes, and satisfy point 3.4 to 3.7 for the child's name; for a delegated parent, they pass the strict grant step against the parent's record; its `exp` is no later than the caller's; `iat ≤ nbf`; `exp − nbf ≤ 24 h`; its depth is at most 8.
- **I2. The tier.** Every issuer-signed token a node accepts satisfies every constraint of point 3, and so does every delegation `apply` records, whoever proposed it.
- **I3. Lineage.** For every recorded delegation `d` minted from the caller's copy `c`: `d.sub = c.sub + "." + segment`, `d.parent = c.sub`, `d.owner = c.owner`, and `d.chain = c.chain ++ [{c.jti, c.kid}]`; for a delegated caller, `apply` checks these against `c`'s record.
- **I4. Ordering.** For every revocation `r` and every `RecordDelegation` `d` in one log: if `r` comes first and covers `d`'s caller (its token ID, a chain `id` or `kid`, its `kid`, or its principal or an ancestor with the caller's `iat` within `before`, plus the leeway for a root caller), `d` is refused; if `d` comes first and `r` names `d`'s caller, a token or key in its chain, or its principal or an ancestor, then `r` covers `d` too, by `d`'s chain or by `d.iat ≤ r.before`. No order, and no pair of equal command times, leaves a live child of a revoked parent. And no delegation is recorded whose token nodes would refuse at once: a child whose `iat`, in whole seconds, falls within a covering principal entry's `before` is refused as retryable (point 11.5).
- **I5. Subtree revocation (release blocker).** Say a token *descends from* a token copy `u` when `u`'s `{jti, kid}` is in its chain, and from a delegation when that delegation's ID is in its chain. Once a revocation is applied, a node that has received it refuses:
  - for `Revoked::Token(t)`: every copy of `t` and every token that descends from the delegation or root token `t`;
  - for `Revoked::Key(k)`: every token `k` signed and every token that descends from a copy `k` signed;
  - for `Revoked::Principal(Q, before)`: every token naming `Q` or a name below it, as `sub` or as an ancestor, with `iat` at or before `before` (plus 30 seconds for a root token);

  whatever keys signed which copies, across issuer rotation and leader changes, and whichever entries replaced which (point 11.4). The tests include a reused segment: `42.c7` revoked by ID, delegated again, with a grandchild under each, then the parent's principal revoked; no replacement revives either grandchild. Every live data node receives it within one heartbeat interval, and refuses all tokens once its list is older than the heartbeat timeout, or holds an entry it can't read; open streams end within `STREAM_RECHECK` after that. **Precondition for control nodes:** a control node verifies tokens against its own applied state, which `auth::boundary` treats as always current. Under Raft, a voter cut off from the group holds stale state indefinitely, so a control node must bound the age of its applied state the same way: a leader counts its freshness from the last time a quorum acknowledged it, and a follower from the last append or heartbeat it accepted from the current leader; past the heartbeat timeout, either refuses every token. **In 0.8,** with one control node and no Raft group, that node's applied state is the fleet's, so it is always current, and `Source::Control` keeps reading it as today. FR-090 adds the freshness bound as a typed hook on `Source::Control` (a freshness the control role reports, which the single writer always reports as current), and FR-064 fills it in from the leader's quorum acknowledgements and the follower's accepted appends; a control node of a Raft group never runs without it.
- **I6. Forged lineage.** No delegation is recorded, and no ancestor-rule revocation is applied, for a delegated caller unless the caller's delegation ID is recorded and live and its presented claims are the recorded claims.
- **I7. Retries.** The same operation, requester, and canonical request yield the same delegation ID and identical claims, and record once; another requester gets `PERMISSION_DENIED` with nothing revealed; a changed request gets `ALREADY_EXISTS`; an expired or revoked delegation is never re-signed or re-minted.
- **I8. No bytes at rest.** No token is ever in control state, a log line, an error, or a metric label.
- **I9. The connection rule.** A token is accepted only on a connection with a client certificate, never on one without. An issuer-signed token is accepted only when the certificate names its `sub` or an ancestor of `sub`, and an authority-signed token only when the certificate names its `sub` itself, or in either case relay-carried (point 4.1). `DelegateToken` and ancestor-rule revocations accept a token only on a connection the first two rules allow, never relay-carried, except that a relay-carried token may revoke its own `jti`.
- **I10. The ancestor rule's reach.** Every entry added without `fleet` was added by a caller that is a root agent with an authority-signed token, or a delegated agent whose recorded claims it presented; and the entry names that caller's presented token, the caller's own principal, or a delegation recorded below the caller, or that delegation's principal. So without `fleet` and without the authority's key, no caller can add an entry that refuses a token outside its own subtree, or outside the subtree of the token it presented; and a stolen issuer key alone can add none.
- **I11. Bounded denylist and records.** Per root lineage: at most 1,024 live `Ancestor` entries the cap counts (*amended 2026-11-13 by the owner, R3 finding 1, option b:* 1,024 the root adds and 1,024 its descendants add between them, so 2,048 plus exempt entries, and about 200 KiB per lineage at both caps rather than the 100 KiB below), plus the root's own principal entry, plus one entry for each unexpired root token the authority issued to the root that the root revoked itself. Each entry is kept no longer than the longest `kept_until` it covers plus 30 seconds (root-token entries: the token's life plus 30 seconds and the 1-hour margin). Principal entries are one per `(fleet, principal)`. Records and operations stay within point 6.3's bounds, and unpruned records never reach the 8,192 backstop under the rates, ticking or not. With entries of at most about 100 bytes, a lineage at its cap adds about 100 KiB to every heartbeat reply.

## Security analysis: what each compromise reaches, and what each revocation ends

This section answers the independent review's two questions directly. "The agent tier" means every database whose name starts with `agent-<id>-` or `agent-<id>.` for some agent ID.

### A stolen issuer key

- **What it can sign.** Tokens every node accepts that satisfy point 3: any delegated agent name under any root, scopes inside that root's namespace, any actions within them (`admin`, `fork`, `stream`, `grant` below the child), for at most 24 hours each, as many as it likes. Nothing else: no root agent name (an issuer-signed token must be a delegated agent name), no `fleet`, no pattern outside an `agent-<root>` namespace, no node or client certificate, and nothing the authority signs.
- **What using them needs.** A forged token for a name under root `42` is usable on a direct connection only with `agent-42`'s certificate key, since no other certificate can name it or an ancestor; it reaches one root's namespace per stolen root certificate. Relay-carried, it needs no certificate: through a router, forged tokens open database sessions on any agent-tier database, reading and writing within the forged scopes. Calls the router doesn't relay (creating, dropping, forking) need a direct connection, or a relay that forwards them.
- **What it can't do.** Obtain recorded descendants, or revoke anything under the ancestor rule: a forged token's delegation ID isn't recorded, and a real ID with claims the control plane didn't record is refused, in `apply` (points 5.9 and 11.3). So setting a forged token's `jti` to another lineage's root token ID, or an operator's token ID, revokes nothing. If it obtains a real delegated token, it can re-sign that token's exact claims, which is just another copy of the same delegation; delegating or revoking from that copy needs a direct connection with its root's certificate key, and every descendant minted from it names the stolen key in its chain.
- **How it ends.** `Revoked::Key(kid)`, kept for ever, refuses every token the key signed, forged or legitimate, and every token minted from a copy it signed. Legitimate delegations whose only copies it signed end with it; a retry or a renew on another control node issues a new copy or a new delegation. The control node gets a new key with `cert issuer`. Unnoticed, the key's reach ends when its certificate expires, within 90 days.

### A compromised control node

- **Its issuer key,** as above.
- **What a control node already decides.** As the leader, it can append any control command, so it can record delegations naming callers whose tokens it never saw. `apply` still holds every record, on every replica, to point 3's tier, point 5.9's structure, and, for a delegated parent, the parent's recorded owner, chain, scopes, and lifetime. Its records carry the `recorded_by` it stamped, which is advisory: it could stamp another control node's ID (point 6.1). A root parent has no record, so a planted root-level child can name a fabricated root token ID in its chain. It also builds the denylist every data node receives in heartbeat replies, so it can withhold revocations from data nodes until it is removed. As a follower in a Raft group (ADR-0035), it can't commit alone, but it can sign with its issuer key and answer reads falsely; with I6 and point 11.3, what it signs can't delegate or revoke. It already decides membership, placement, and leases; delegation adds the agent-tier tokens above to that.
- **A variant to know.** A record it fabricates can name an honest control node's `kid` for the caller's copy, or a fabricated root token ID with the authority's `kid`, in its chain. Its own key signed the copies it handed out, so `Revoked::Key` of its key ends those, but an honest control node will re-sign a recorded delegation on a retry from the recorded requester, and that copy and its descendants don't carry the compromised key. So `Revoked::Key` alone doesn't undo what it recorded.
- **What it can't do.** It never holds the authority's key, so it can't mint root tokens or certificates, and an issuer-signed token never leaves the agent tier on an honest node.
- **How it ends.** Remove it from the control group, revoke its issuer key (`Revoked::Key`) from an honest leader, and give the replacement a new key. **Every delegation recorded while it may have led is suspect:** list them by the whole window it may have led, `agent list --fleet --since <time>`, not only by `--recorded-by <node-id>` (point 6.4), and revoke their lineages by principal (`Revoked::Principal` of each affected root agent, or of each delegated principal listed), which ends them whatever keys signed their copies. Its node certificate is handled as for any compromised node.

### A relay's certificate key

- A router, or anything holding a `forward_tokens` principal's certificate key, can carry every token it is handed, and replay any it has seen, for those tokens' scopes until they expire or are revoked. That is true of every token today (ADR-0037's "a compromised router" consequence).
- It can revoke a token it was handed by that token's own `jti`, ending only that token and its descendants: a denial of service it could cause anyway by dropping requests. It can't delegate, or make any other ancestor-rule revocation, with the tokens it carries (point 4.5).

### A root agent's certificate key

- Alone, nothing: `agent-<id>` has no `[[principal]]` entry.
- With a token of its lineage, the root's own or any descendant's: that token on a direct connection, delegation from it within its `grant`, and the ancestor rule's revocations within the subtree of that token's principal. It is what makes a stolen child's or parent's token usable directly, so it is held like the root's token.

### A child's stolen token (for example `agent-42.c7`'s)

- **On a direct connection:** nothing, without `agent-42`'s certificate key. It is refused with `agent-43`'s certificate, with a node certificate, and with any certificate that isn't an ancestor's; no certificate for a dotted agent name exists.
- **Relay-carried:** the child's own scopes, for database sessions, until it expires (at most 24 hours, 2 hours by default) or is revoked. It can't delegate (point 4.5).
- **With the root's certificate key too:** the child's scopes, plus minting grandchildren if it holds `grant`, each recorded below the child and within its ceiling, and revocations within the child's subtree.
- **On the child's own machine, in trusted local development** (ADR-0044 §11), a process running as the same user can read the root's directory, so its certificate key and its token. Delegation narrows what a credential presented to a node can do; it doesn't isolate processes of one user. Every command that delegates says so.
- **How it ends.** `Revoked::Token` of its delegation ID (every copy), `Revoked::Principal` of the child or any ancestor, the root's token ID, or the key of any issuer that signed a copy in its lineage.

### A parent's stolen token

- **Relay-carried:** the parent's scopes for database sessions, until it expires or is revoked; no delegation.
- **With its root's certificate key:** the parent's scopes, plus minting children within the parent's scopes and lifetime, at most 30 a minute (60 from the lineage's delegated agents, which never stops the root) and 256 live per root, each recorded with the parent in its lineage, plus revocations within the parent's subtree, within point 11.6's cap.
- **How it ends.** Revoking the parent's token ID ends it and, through their chains, every child minted from it. `Revoked::Principal` of the parent ends every token of the parent issued until then and every descendant of any of them, including children minted from tokens the revoker never saw. The parent itself, an ancestor, or an operator with `fleet` may do either (point 11). Either revocation prunes the records it ends, so their slots free at once, and replaces whatever entries the thief added below the parent, so it is never refused for the cap.

### Can any revocation leave part of the subtree alive?

For each variant, "the subtree" is what point 13's I5 says the variant names.

- **`Revoked::Token` of a root token or a delegation:** no. Every descendant carries the ID in its chain, set when it was minted from the caller's signed claims, whatever key signed any copy, and `apply` holds a delegated caller's chain to its record. Every copy of a delegation has that ID as its `jti`. A delegation the log orders after the revocation is refused (I4). This doesn't depend on any clock. Under the ancestor rule it depends on the delegation's record being kept, which point 6.1 keeps for the token's whole life plus a margin.
- **`Revoked::Key`:** no, for what the key signed and what was minted from its signatures. It doesn't end copies of the same delegations that other keys signed, where any exist, or their descendants: those aren't below the key, they are below the delegation, and ending a delegation is `Revoked::Token` of its ID or `Revoked::Principal`. FR-090's test checks exactly this split: after `42.c7` is re-signed by B, `Revoked::Token(c7)` ends both grandchildren, and `Revoked::Key(A)` ends only the one minted from A's copy.
- **`Revoked::Principal`:** no, for every delegated token below a covered token. A delegated token's `iat` is control time, monotonic in log order, so each descendant recorded before the revocation has `iat ≤ before`, and none can be recorded after it from a covered caller (I4). For a root token, it holds within the clock bound under "Assumptions"; `Revoked::Token` of the root's ID has no such bound. A principal revocation of the caller itself needs no record but the caller's.
- **Across replacement:** an entry that replaces others covers everything they covered (point 11.4) and is kept at least as long, so replacing never revives a token. Root-token ID entries and `Fleet` entries are never replaced.
- **Across issuer rotation and leader changes:** revocations, delegations, and their order live in the one replicated control log, which a new leader holds whole; chains name each copy's key, so no check depends on which control node signed or which key it holds now.
- **Across pruning:** nodes check chains and the denylist, not `State::delegations`, so pruning a record changes no node's decision. Records are pruned early only when a revocation already covers them. Records and ancestor-rule entries are kept for durations counted from their commands' effective times, plus a 1-hour margin, so neither expires before what it covers under the clock assumption below. A child expires no later than its parent, so its record goes first.
- **Across a lost reply, a crash, or a retry:** a delegation committed but never signed has no token; one signed twice by two keys is one delegation ID, covered by every revocation of it.
- **Across a node that can't read an entry:** it refuses every token until it can (point 3), so a new denylist variant never silently fails open on an older node.

### Assumptions, and what remains

- **Propagation is ADR-0026's,** with I5's precondition for control nodes. A connected data node refuses a revoked token, or any token below it, within one heartbeat interval; one cut off from the control plane honours it for at most the heartbeat timeout (5 s by default), then refuses every token. The router keeps no denylist, and data nodes decide (ADR-0037 point 18).
- **Control time doesn't run more than 1 hour ahead of the clocks nodes check tokens by.** Records and ancestor-rule entries are kept for their tokens' lives plus 1 hour, counted in control time. A leader whose clock steps forward later by more than that margin can make pruning forget an entry, and the record that would let an agent revoke the token again, while the token is still valid by the nodes' clocks. The handler's refusal to mint while control time leads its clock by more than 30 seconds (point 5.4), and the warnings a control node logs when its clock and control time disagree, make such a step visible; `fleet` can always revoke again by token ID or key.
- **A principal revocation of a root token assumes bounded skew of the signer's clock.** `Revoked::Principal` judges a root token by the `iat` its signer wrote with the authority's key, as ADR-0044 §3.8 already decided. The assumption is that **the clock of the host that signs root tokens is at most 30 seconds ahead of control time**, the leeway nodes add for root tokens (point 3). In the other direction, a signer's clock behind control time causes only refusals: a root token minted after a principal revocation by a lagging signer can carry an `iat` within `before` plus 30 seconds, and is refused until the entry is forgotten (30 days), so the operator mints it again from an accurate clock, after the time the revoking command printed. If the signer's clock is more than 30 seconds ahead, a root token issued before the revocation can escape it and keep delegating; delegated tokens aren't affected. The clock-free handle for a root token is its ID: `agent remove <root-id>` revokes it too (point 11.7), `agent renew <root-id>` revokes the ID it replaces, and an operator can revoke a root token's ID or the authority's key.
- **A revocation is only as good as the control plane.** A compromised leader can withhold revocations from data nodes until it is removed, and what it recorded is listed by the window it led, `recorded_by` being advisory, and revoked by principal (above).
- **Trusted local development isn't isolation.** Until the agent launcher (ADR-0044 §11, with ADR-0048's OpenShell backend proposed for it), a child process can read its root's credentials. Under that launcher no sandbox holds a token or the root's certificate (ADR-0048 point 2), so a sandboxed child can't, and its token is relay-carried (point 4.6).
- **A relay-carried token is a bearer token.** ADR-0044's umbrella asks that copied bytes be inert. That holds on direct connections. Relay-carried, a delegated token is usable for its own scopes, within at most 24 hours, as S1 also allowed for bearer delegations through the router. A fleet that runs no router, or binds it to loopback, has no relay.
- **The denylist's size scales with active root lineages.** Each lineage adds at most about 100 KiB (I11), so a fleet would need several hundred root agents, each at its cap at once, to approach the 64 MiB message limit. Root agents exist only where the authority issued them. Sending denylist changes rather than the whole list, which ADR-0026 deferred, is the remedy if a fleet grows that far.
- **A malformed entry stops every token.** A control node that sends an entry no data node can read makes every data node refuse every token until it is fixed. That is the chosen failure: closed, loud, and visible in the logs.
- **The ADR-0026 point 5 exception stands.** A root token whose `nbf` its signer set far in the future can outlive its ID's revocation; its key's revocation is the remedy, as today.

## Alternatives considered

- **Offline biscuit delegation (ADR-0044 S1), now.** It lets a parent delegate with the control plane unreachable, from any language. In 0.8 a child can't use its databases with the control plane unreachable anyway, and S1 costs a dependency of about 85 crates (34 new to the workspace, with duplicate versions of `prost`, `sha2`, `base64`, and `rand`), holder keys, a new TLS setting that makes the token parser every exposed node's first filter, a verifier of our own, and two new revocation variants. **Deferred,** with the evidence that would bring it back in point 9.6.
- **Online signing with the authority's key.** No new key or certificate, but the authority's key would have to be on control nodes, which ADR-0025 point 2 forbids, and a stolen copy would reach everything. Rejected.
- **Issuer keys listed in every node's configuration** (`[tls] issuers`) instead of `x5c`. Tokens would be smaller, but every node would need a configuration change and a reload to add or rotate an issuer, and a stale list would refuse good tokens or accept a retired key. `x5c` needs only the authority bundle nodes already hold. Rejected.
- **No root-namespace constraint on issuer-signed tokens,** so a parent could hand a child any scope it can grant, such as `read:pub-*`. More flexible, but a stolen issuer key would reach everything below `fleet`. Rejected; the cost is that a child receives only scopes inside its root's namespace, which covers 0.8's workflows (the root's memory, the child's own databases).
- **One issuer key shared by every control node.** Signatures wouldn't change after a leader change, but the private key would be copied between machines, and revoking it would end every delegation in the fleet. Per-node keys, with keys named in the chain, keep a key's revocation narrow and still revoke a subtree as one. Rejected.
- **Issuing a client certificate for each child.** Children could run on other machines, but the control plane would hold a certificate-issuing key, and 0.7's refusal of dotted names in certificates would be reversed. Rejected for 0.8; holder keys are the deferred answer to remote children.
- **Accepting a delegated token on any authenticated connection.** Simplest, but a stolen child's token would work with any certificate the fleet issued. Rejected.
- **Storing the issued token bytes so a retry returns identical bytes.** Control state is replicated, snapshotted, and backed up, so it would hold bearer credentials wherever those copies go. Rejected for re-signing recorded claims.
- **Minting a fresh delegation on every retry.** No record of the operation needed, but a lost reply would leave a live delegation nobody holds, counted against the limits, and a crash-and-rerun would make two. Rejected.
- **Keeping operation records for 7 days, as forks do, with a cap.** It would let a caller fill its lineage's cap in hours and then be locked out for a week. Records last as long as their delegation plus an hour, which the rates bound. Rejected.
- **A chain of IDs without key IDs.** Revoking an issuer key would then end only the tokens it signed directly. A stolen key can sign a valid copy of a real delegation it has seen, and that copy can delegate; without its `kid` in the descendants' chains, they would survive the key's revocation. Rejected.
- **Revocation decided from control state** (nodes look up lineage, or the control plane expands a revoked parent into its descendants' IDs). Nodes would need delegation records, or the denylist would grow with every subtree and miss descendants recorded later. The chain makes the check local, offline, and complete. Rejected.
- **Skipping denylist entries a node can't read,** as 0.7 does. A node that missed a new variant would accept what it should refuse. Rejected for refusing every token, as for a stale list.
- **An ancestor rule open to every principal, or trusting a delegated caller's token alone.** Simple, but a pre-0.7 service such as `workload` could end `workload.prod`'s tokens, and a token forged with a stolen issuer key, its `jti` set to any token ID it has seen, could revoke another lineage's or an operator's token. Rejected: agents only, recorded callers checked in `apply`, recorded targets.
- **Exempting every self-revocation from the cap.** It let a lineage delegate and self-revoke in a loop without bound. Rejected: only a root's own revocations, which replace or raise a bounded set of entries, are exempt.
- **A second, text-level scope checker inside `apply`,** with a differential test against the lattice. It would freeze a second implementation into the log. Rejected for moving the one pure implementation into `fleetring-control` and gating its changes as fleet features (point 6.5).
- **A 30-second leeway on every `before`.** It also refused delegated tokens minted just after a revocation, whose `iat` is control time and needs none. Rejected for applying the leeway to root tokens only.
- **Requiring `iat ≤ nbf` of every token.** It would break tokens other JWT libraries mint with a backdated `nbf`, without a gate. Rejected for refusing an `iat` in the future (point 2.7).
- **Capping an explicit lifetime silently.** A caller would get less than it asked for without noticing. Only the default is capped, and the response says so. Rejected.
- **OAuth 2.0 token exchange (RFC 8693).** This design is close to it in spirit: a subject token in, a narrower token out. Speaking it would add an HTTP endpoint and its vocabulary beside the gRPC control plane, for no client that needs it. Rejected for 0.8.
- **A delegation tool in the MCP server.** It would put minting, and a token, within the model's reach. Rejected (point 7).
- **Accepting relay-carried tokens for `DelegateToken` and the ancestor rule.** It would let a relay, or anyone with a copied token and a relay, mint children or fill the denylist. Rejected (point 4.5), except for a token revoking its own `jti`.

## Consequences

- A parent agent gives a child a narrower, shorter-lived credential in one command, and revokes it, or the child's whole subtree, without the `fleet` scope.
- The token format grows by an optional header field and four optional claims, and nodes verify one certificate per delegated token. JWTs stay the only token format, with no new dependency. FleetRing's signers write `iat` as the signing time, and nodes refuse an `iat` in the future beyond the leeway.
- Each control node holds a new online signing key. Its reach is confined to the agent tier on every node, it is revocable, and its certificate lasts 30 days by default and 90 at most. Operators run `fleetring cert issuer` per control node, configure two paths, and renew the certificate as they renew node certificates.
- `SubjectUri` gains the issuer kind, and `peer_of` refuses it. `TokenSpec` gains `kind`, `owner`, `parent`, `chain`, and an issue time apart from `not_before`; a `DelegationSigner` signs with `x5c`; the verifier gains the issuer path and the checks of point 3, on the request path and the stream recheck alike; and `resolve_identified` refuses a token on a connection with no client certificate (point 4.1). The router, which verifies tokens with the same verifier, accepts delegated tokens with no change of its own.
- The scope grammar and lattice move into `fleetring-control`, and changes to them that change `apply`'s outcomes become fleet features.
- A data node that can't read a denylist entry refuses every token instead of skipping it, and a control node bounds the age of its own applied state (I5).
- Control state gains `delegations`, `delegation_operations`, and `rate_windows`, bounded per root lineage by the 256-live limit and the rates (point 6.3), and `Revocations` entries record how they were added, so that `apply` can enforce the ancestor rule's cap and rates. The control log gains `RecordDelegation`, behind the `delegation` feature with `Revoked::Principal` and the ancestor rule. `apply` checks principal names, token IDs, and scopes that commands carry (ADR-0025 point 5, as amended). The replay test covers all of it.
- `RevokeToken` is no longer `fleet`-only for agents: the ancestor rule lets an agent revoke what it and its descendants issued, and itself, within bounds that keep the denylist finite per root lineage and that no descendant can use to block an ancestor.
- `Control.ListDelegations` (with `fleet`) lists live records by recording leader and time window, for incident response.
- Root agents' tokens gain `kind`, `owner`, and `grant` once the fleet runs 0.8, so 0.7 nodes refuse them, which ADR-0038's window makes safe.
- `identity.toml` version 2 (shared with FR-081's `scopes_from`) carries a delegated identity's lineage. A 0.7 CLI refuses it with "upgrade".
- A child runs on the machine its root's certificate is on. Remote children wait for holder keys or enrollment.
- Delegation is trusted local development until the agent launcher exists (ADR-0044 §11; ADR-0048 proposes its first backend), and every command that delegates prints the trusted-local-development line. Under the launcher, the launcher delegates, and sandboxed agents reach the fleet only through the router (point 4.6).
- **Docs:** [`docs/cli.md`](../cli.md) §16 designs `cert issuer`, `agent delegate`, lineage in `agent list` and `agent show`, `agent list --fleet`, `agent renew` and `agent remove` for children, and `token revoke --principal`, planned for 0.8.0. The [lexicon](../lexicon.md) gains *delegated agent*, *delegation issuer* (with *issuer certificate*), *delegation ID*, and *lineage*, planned; *delegation* is redefined as online; *holder key*, *revocation ID*, and the biscuit notes are marked deferred after 0.8. ADR-0025, ADR-0026, ADR-0039, ADR-0043, and ADR-0044 carry dated notes, and `SECURITY.md` gains the issuer, the compromise table, and the connection rule with FR-090.
- **Follow-up work:** FR-090 (the issuer, node checks, and the control node's state-age bound), FR-091 (`DelegateToken`, lineage in control state, `ListDelegations`, and moving the scope lattice), FR-092 (the CLI and root agents that can delegate), FR-093 (the ancestor rule, `Revoked::Principal`, entries that record how they were added, the unreadable-denylist rule, `iat` as signing time, and reporting `delegation`), FR-094 (the evidence for point 9.6), and FR-096 (the upgrade from 0.7). FR-090's ticket says nodes report `delegation` from that PR; point 6.6 moves that to FR-093, and the ticket is updated to match.

## Open questions for the owner

*Decided 2026-11-12:* the owner accepted questions 1 to 6 as recommended below.

The ticket settles O1 and O2 as recommended, (a) and (a), and the body is written with them. An independent security review, its re-review, and a re-check of the revised text (2026-11-12) accepted the design with changes, all folded in above. They agreed with questions 1 to 4 and 6 as recommended, except that revoked delegations shouldn't count toward the 256, and preferred 5(b). Each is written in as recommended below.

1. **Relay-carried tokens can't delegate or use the ancestor rule** (point 4.5). (a) `DelegateToken` and ancestor-rule revocations refuse them, so both need an ancestor's certificate key, except that a token may revoke its own `jti` through a relay; (b) accept them, as every other call does. *Recommended: (a).*
2. **Forged lineage is checked by claims, not only by ID, for delegation and revocation alike** (points 5.9, 11.3). (a) A delegated caller's presented claims must equal the recorded claims for its delegation ID, checked in `apply`; (b) "recorded and live" alone, which lets a stolen issuer key widen a real delegation's scopes, within the tier, and delegate from it. *Recommended: (a).*
3. **The separator refusal on nodes** (point 3.7). (a) Nodes refuse an issuer-signed token whose pattern ends before `*` in a letter or digit, as ADR-0044 §2.3 asks of new token forms; (b) leave it to the handler. *Recommended: (a).*
4. **A root agent removing itself also revokes its own token ID** (point 11.7), so the subtree ends independently of the signer's clock. *Recommended: yes.*
5. **How long operation records last** (points 5.8, 6.2). (a) 7 days, capped at 10,000 per root lineage, which can lock a busy lineage out for a week; (b) until the delegation's record goes, plus 1 hour, bounded by the rates; (c) 7 days, unbounded. *Recommended: (b).*
6. **Smaller choices:**
   - a 60-second minimum explicit lifetime, and no `not_before` more than 30 seconds in the past (point 5.4);
   - revoked delegations stop counting toward the 256, since their records are pruned (points 5.10, 11.4);
   - a retry re-signs only a live, unrevoked delegation (point 5.7);
   - `token revoke --principal` asks for the name to be typed, as `--key-id` does (`docs/cli.md` §7);
   - an authority-signed `kind = "agent"` token must name a root agent (point 2.6);
   - issuer certificates last at most 90 days, and dev mode replaces its own (point 1);
   - the ancestor rule adds at most 1,024 counted entries per root lineage; revocations and delegations are each limited to 30 a minute per caller and 60 a minute from a lineage's delegated agents, the root never counted in the latter (points 5.10, 11.6);
   - `not_before` at most 24 hours ahead, and a same-second re-delegation of a revoked name refused as retryable (points 5.4, 11.5);
   - `before` is stored as the effective time, and nodes add 30 seconds only for root tokens (point 11.5);
   - the scope grammar and lattice move into `fleetring-control`, rather than `apply` getting a second checker (point 6.5);
   - `Control.ListDelegations`, with `fleet`, lists records by recording leader and by time window, `recorded_by` being advisory against a compromised leader (points 6.1, 6.4);
   - a tombstone lasts 1 hour from its revocation, and a `fleet` revocation's `until` is never earlier than the records it prunes need (points 6.2, 11.5);
   - a token on a connection with no client certificate is refused, and only issuer-signed tokens use the ancestor match (point 4.1);
   - a backstop of 8,192 unpruned records per root lineage, which pruning in `apply` and the rates keep from ever firing (point 6.3).

   *Recommended: all as written.*
